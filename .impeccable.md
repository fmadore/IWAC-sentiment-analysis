# Design Context — IWAC Sentiment Analysis Dashboard

## Users

Academic researchers — specialists in African studies, Islamic studies, and digital humanities. They are comfortable with dense, information-rich interfaces and value methodological rigor over hand-holding. The dashboard is a research instrument, not a marketing surface.

Use context: long analytical sessions, often comparing multiple AI models against the same corpus. Researchers will copy data out (CSV exports), drill into individual articles, cite findings in publications. Bilingual French/English audience working with francophone West African press.

## Brand Personality

**Investigative · serious · contemporary.**

Reference language: Reuters Graphics, FT Visual Journalism, Bellingcat, The Pudding's serious investigations, Bloomberg Graphics (the editorial side, not the terminal). Dense charts foregrounded. Confidence in the data. Restrained chrome.

The interface should feel like a piece of investigative journalism that happens to be interactive — not a SaaS product, not a research portal, not a museum exhibit.

## Aesthetic Direction

- **Theme: dark only.** Long sessions, charts pop, signals analytical work in progress.
- **Density over decoration.** Information density is a feature for this audience.
- **Anti-reference: generic SaaS dashboard.** No purple→blue gradients. No "elevate everything in a card." No identical card grids. No glassmorphism for its own sake. No emoji in chrome.
- **Charts are the primary content,** not accents. Filters and chrome serve the data.
- **Typography carries the editorial voice** — distinctive display face for headings, neutral but well-set body face, tabular numerals everywhere data appears.

## Design Principles

1. **Foregrounded data, restrained chrome.** Every UI surface earns its weight against the chart it surrounds. Borders, dividers, and shadows work below visibility unless they convey meaning.
2. **Editorial typography.** Type hierarchy reads first; layouts read second. Headings, eyebrows (kickers), and figure captions should feel set, not styled.
3. **Methodological transparency.** The dashboard is a research artefact. Methodology, sample sizes, model versions, and uncertainty must be visible — not hidden in tooltips.
4. **Tabular precision.** Numerals are tabular. Sentiment categories use a stable, perceptually-ordered palette. Comparison views align rather than juxtapose.
5. **Bilingual parity.** French and English are first-class; layouts must hold up to ~30% expansion without breaking.
6. **No SaaS tells.** Reject the LinearStripeNotion visual vocabulary on sight: rainbow icon backgrounds, gradient pill buttons, "primary CTA" hierarchies, marketing-page hero panels.
